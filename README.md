## If you're a Pi miner: getting your mined PI onto OKX

A lot of people searching "where to buy Pi Network" are actually Pioneers who've been mining for years and want to know how to get their existing balance onto an exchange to sell. The flow is different from a clean buy.

### Complete KYC inside the Pi app

Pi Network has its own KYC process, separate from OKX's. Until you pass it, your mined balance sits on the testnet and can't move anywhere. This is the single biggest blocker for most long-time miners — KYC backlog has been an issue since the mainnet launch, and some users have waited months for approval.

### Migrate to the Open Mainnet

Once KYC is done, you go through the **Mainnet Checklist** inside the Pi app. The key steps:

1. Complete Pi Network KYC verification
2. Create a Mainnet-compatible Pi Wallet through the Pi Browser
3. Save your 24-word seed phrase offline
4. Confirm your Mainnet wallet address (steps 3 and 6 of the checklist)
5. Wait for migration to complete — only migrated, unlocked PI can be sent externally

Pi Core Team has been running migration in batches since Open Mainnet, and second-round migrations have continued through 2026. If your migration hasn't gone through yet, there's not much you can do besides wait and make sure your checklist is complete.

### Check for locked vs. unlocked PI

Pi Network lets users voluntarily lock up coins for future rewards. Locked coins are non-transferable — you can't send them to OKX or anywhere else until the lockup expires. Lockups can run years; some users report coins locked until late 2027. Before you try to deposit, confirm the coins you want to move are actually unlocked.

## Things that catch people out

A few things that aren't always obvious from exchange listing pages:

**PI is not on any decentralized exchange.** If you see "Pi" on Uniswap, PancakeSwap, or anywhere else in a DEX interface, it's not the real Pi Network token. Pi Network's official guidance is to treat any DEX-listed "PI" as suspect. The real token only moves on Pi Network's mainnet and only trades on the CEXs that have done the mainnet integration.

**Travel Rule can lock your deposit.** EEA users depositing PI into OKX have run into deposits stuck in "pending" status because of Travel Rule verification. The fix is to go through the deposit unlock flow and verify the source of the funds. It's annoying but it's a regulatory requirement, not a bug.

**Locked coins can't be sold.** If you're a Pioneer with coins locked for rewards, you can't send them to OKX until the lockup expires. Some lockups run into late 2027. Check your lockup schedule inside the Pi app before assuming your full balance is sellable.

**Regional restrictions are real and shifting.** OKX's PI trading is not available in mainland China and may be restricted in other jurisdictions. Even within the US, product availability varies by state. Check the pair on your OKX instance before assuming you can trade.

**The price has been brutal.** PI's all-time high of $3.00 was set on February 27, 2025, a week after listing. As of late August 2026, the price is around $0.091 — down roughly 97%. Circulating supply has grown to about 11.1 billion PI out of a max supply of 100 billion, and the project has scheduled token unlocks that add supply pressure through 2026 and beyond. None of this is hidden information, but it's worth internalizing before you buy in expecting a quick recovery to ATH.

**P2P Pi deals are a bad idea.** During the enclosed mainnet phase, peer-to-peer Pi trades were common because no CEX supported the coin. Now that OKX and other regulated exchanges list real mainnet PI, P2P is mostly a scam vector — fake tokens, impersonators, no escrow, no recourse. If someone on Telegram or a Facebook group offers to sell you PI directly, treat it as fraud unless proven otherwise.
